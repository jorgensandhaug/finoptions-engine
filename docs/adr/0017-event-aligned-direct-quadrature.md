# ADR 0017: Event-aligned direct quadrature for finite call exercise schedules

- Status: accepted for implementation and convergence validation
- Date: 2026-07-15

## Context

The independently fitted LSM estimator needs a deterministic cross-method check on the same
one-dimensional market model and declared finite exercise schedule. A cash-dividend call is not
made recombining merely by inserting a fixed cash jump into a conventional tree. The engine also
has to preserve its existing event order: exercise observes the stock's left limit, a declared cash
jump follows, and an admitted terminal cash jump precedes expiry payoff.

Direct integration provides an independent backward valuation route when the transition law between
event nodes is known. For constant-volatility risk-neutral GBM, each continuation expectation is a
one-dimensional standard-normal integral. Gauss-Hermite quadrature evaluates that integral on an
explicit stock grid; interpolation maps both stochastic transitions and deterministic cash jumps
back to the grid.

## Decision

Add a separately named event-aligned quadrature crate. Its first instrument is a fixed-strike call
under the existing exact event schedule, node log-discount curve, cumulative nonnegative continuous
dividend-yield curve, constant positive volatility, and explicitly selected liquidator or survivor
cash policy.

The caller must provide all numerical controls:

- a finite upper stock boundary strictly above both initial spot and strike;
- at least 1,001 uniformly spaced stock points, including exact zero and the upper boundary;
- one version-pinned Gauss-Hermite order from `32, 64, 128, 256, 512, 1,024, 2,048`; and
- an explicit `CpuExecutor`, including worker count and exponential implementation.

The checked-in rules are generated by SciPy 1.18.0's `roots_hermitenorm`, normalized to standard
normal mass, and stored as exact `f64` bit patterns. Underflowed zero-weight tail nodes are omitted;
they make no contribution in `f64`. Runtime construction mirrors the positive half exactly and
validates mass, mean, variance, ordering, finiteness, positivity, and symmetry before pricing.
Backward transitions use piecewise-linear
interpolation on the uniform grid and an explicit affine high-stock continuation boundary. The
boundary slope is multiplied by the proportional-payout factor and the intercept by the interval
discount factor. A cash jump changes the intercept by minus slope times the cash amount. At an
exercise node, the continuation and call intrinsic value are compared pointwise before the cash
jump. At expiry, an admitted cash jump is applied before the terminal payoff.

No boundary, grid, quadrature order, exercise node, dividend event, dividend policy, discount,
yield, volatility, or backend is inferred or changed. Allocation failure, invalid quadrature,
non-finite transition, non-finite value, and grid mismatch are typed failures. There is no retry,
adaptive refinement, clipping, alternate interpolation, alternate backend, or silent fallback.

## Validation contract

Production budgets are not defaults. A study must predeclare refinements of upper boundary, stock
spacing, and quadrature order and retain the complete sequence. Tests cover standard-normal moments,
Black-Scholes European reductions with and without continuous yield, zero-dividend American-call
identities in their theorem domain, terminal-cash payoff ordering, published one-dividend
liquidator values, financial bounds, serial/parallel equality, and an independent interpolated cash
tree. Cross-method agreement is an error envelope, not exact digit equality and not a requirement
that LSM equal this finite deterministic approximation.

## Consequences

- The deterministic method can validate cash and continuous-yield slices without sharing LSM's
  simulation, regression, stopping-policy, or random-number implementation.
- Terminal cash events no longer require a timing approximation in the deterministic check.
- Linear interpolation is deliberately lower order than the cubic interpolation in the direct
  integration literature; its error is exposed through refinement rather than hidden behind a
  nominal method label.
- The first implementation is one-dimensional and call-specific. It does not imply support for
  stochastic rates, stochastic volatility, continuous-time American exercise, puts, or arbitrary
  payoffs.

## Initial evidence

The permanent tests reproduce Black-Scholes European reductions with and without continuous yield,
the no-dividend American-call identity in its nonnegative-rate domain, exact terminal-liquidator-cash
equivalence to an increased strike, and the published one-dividend liquidator value. The latter is
computed independently by adaptive integration in `finoptions-testkit`; a separate 16,000-step
interpolated CRR tree agrees within `5e-5`. Serial and dedicated-pool direct integration are bitwise
identical under the portable scalar exponential backend.

On an Apple M4 Pro, the release Python operation for the published one-dividend case with 20,001
stock points measured median wall times of 42.39 ms at order 1,024 on one thread and 10.98 ms on four
threads. Order 2,048 measured 62.54 ms and 15.84 ms. These include Python/native validation,
allocation, all backward transitions, and diagnostics. Apple Accelerate and portable exponentials
were effectively tied because each reusable method object owns its validated rule and each price
requires only one short multiplier exponential vector; neither backend is selected automatically.
Full protocol and raw endpoints are recorded in the
[performance checkpoint](../performance/2026-07-15-event-aligned-quadrature.md).
